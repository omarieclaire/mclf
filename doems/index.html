Workshops and zines

Work where the people who show up are co-authors rather than audience. For the past decade I've run hands-on workshops and turned what comes out of them into zines other people can keep.

Workshops

A Kind of Play

A game-making workshop I have been running for about a decade. The format is prompts, hands, materials, and a room of people making things at the same time. No prior experience, no coding, nothing to install. Participants build small experimental games out of whatever is on the table and write up the rules, so what they made can be played again by somebody who wasn't there.

Berlin (2017), Johannesburg (2017), Dundee (2018), Kraków (2019), Arles (2022), Barcelona (2025), and twice for the Pixelles Game Incubator in Montreal (2020, 2021).

Personal Games, School of Machines, Making & Make-Believe

I taught a hands-on guide to making personal games with Bahiyya Khan and Lorenzo Pilia, twice, in the spring and fall terms of 2020. Everyone who took it made a game.

Making things with your hands

An alt-controller workshop in 2025, building game controllers out of physical materials rather than buying them. I've also co-hosted shorter, one-off sessions when a smaller format is the better fit — a tiny zine-making workshop with cyberlove at the Open Hardware Summit (2024), for instance, where everyone left with a handmade zine in under an hour.

Collaborative zines

If You Don't Like the Game, Change the Rules (2023)

A comic about worker co-operatives, unions and labour in the games industry, drawn from 88 interviews and survey responses, with research support from Michael Iantorno. It became the seed for a workshop I toured to Mexico City, Berlin, Taipei, Tokyo, Kyoto, San Francisco, Montreal, Halifax and elsewhere.

At every stop, participants with no zine-making experience left holding a zine they had written and drawn themselves, about their own relationship to work — prompts, materials, and everyone credited for what they made. It's the format I bring to every workshop I run. The comic itself exists as an illustrated story and a research whitepaper, in English and French.

Alongside these, I spend an hour of most days on how machines talk to each other, and turn it into zines: DNS, the terminal, Git, and how one computer finds another.

Finding Our Way (2021–22)

A zine that came out of Tech Tech Tech, a project I co-led with Ada X in Montréal exploring alternatives to the tech giants. It involved deep research, interviews, and a four-part workshop with co-researchers looking for alternatives to corporate tools for office work, social media, online gatherings, portfolios, performances and exhibitions. What the co-researchers found is what the zine is made of. A second zine, Living in the Time of Tech Giants, came out of the same project.

Emergency Exhibition Care (2019)

Made during my residency at the NYU Game Center with students and faculty there. A zine and care project where exhibiting student artists could access and choose from a menu of care items.

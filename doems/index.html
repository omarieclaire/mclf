Workshops and zines

I like running participatory sessions where the people who show up are co-authors rather than my audience. For the past decade I've run hands-on workshops and turned what comes out of them info tiny experimental games and/or zines people can keep.

A Kind of Play

A Kind of Play is an experimental game-making workshop I have been running for about a decade. The format is prompts, hands, materials, and a room of people making things at the same time. People don't need any prior experience or experience coding. Participants design small experimental games by exploring their own memories of play and experimenting with everyday objects.

Berlin (2017), Johannesburg (2017), Dundee (2018), Kraków (2019), Arles (2022), Barcelona (2025), and twice for the Pixelles Game Incubator in Montreal (2020, 2021).

Personal Games, School of Machines, Making & Make-Believe

I taught a hands-on guide to making personal games with Bahiyya Khan and Lorenzo Pilia, twice, in the spring and fall terms of 2020. Students made personal games from their own lived experience.

Making things with your hands

I hosted an alt-controller workshop at Dinacon in 2025 in Indonesia, building game controllers out of elecrontics, found materials, trash, and the things that surround us.

Quick mini zines

I've also co-hosted shorter, one-off sessions when a smaller format is the better fit. A tiny zine-making workshop with cyberlove at the Open Hardware Summit (2024), for instance, where everyone made a handmade zine.

Collaborative zines

If You Don't Like the Game, Change the Rules (2023)

I made a comic about worker co-operatives, unions and labour in the games industry, drawn from 88 interviews and survey responses, with research support from Michael Iantorno (read the comic · lire la BD). It became the seed for a workshop I toured to Mexico City, Berlin, Taipei, Tokyo, Kyoto, Barcelona, San Francisco, Montreal, Halifax and elsewhere.

At every stop, participants with no zine-making experience left holding a zine they had written and drawn themselves, about their own relationship to work.

Meshtastic

I made a zine about Meshtastic, a technology that lets us text friends on a decentralized network we build together, sending messages over radio waves, with no wifi, no data plan and no company in the middle — read the zine

Wizard Zines, with Julia Evans

I spend an hour of most days on how machines talk to each other with a longtime friend and collaborator, and we turn it into zines: DNS, the terminal, Git, and how one computer finds another.

Finding Our Way (2021–22)

Finding Our Way is a collaborative zine I compiled and edited, from Tech Tech Tech, a project exploring alternatives to the tech giants. It was made in collaboration with Sarah Choukah, Liane Décary-Chen, and Hannah Strauss, hosted and facilitated by Ada X in Montréal. It involved deep research, interviews, and a four-part workshop with a number of artists and members of artist run centers looking for alternatives to corporate tools for office work, social media, online gatherings, portfolios, performances and exhibitions. I also wrote a second zine from the same project, Living in the Time of Tech Giants.
